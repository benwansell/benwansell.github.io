Political economy of redistribution

Meltzer–Richard Explorer

Set the income distribution, the pivotal voter and the fiscal rules. The model solves for the tax take and benefits that voter prefers, with everyone’s labour supply responding to the taxes chosen.

Designed by Ben Ansell, with the help of Claude Opus 5.5. Based on Meltzer, Allan H., and Scott F. Richard. “A Rational Theory of the Size of Government.” Journal of Political Economy 89, no. 5 (1981): 914–927.

How the model works

Population. Earning power w is lognormal across 400 quantiles. Its spread is set so that market income has the Gini you choose; mean market income at zero tax is normalised to 1.

Preferences. Each person maximises U(c − v(l)) with v(l) = l^(1+1/ε)/(1+1/ε) and CRRA U with coefficient ρ. These GHH preferences have no income effects, so hours depend only on the net marginal wage: l = ((1−t)w)^ε.

Taxes. T(y) = τ · max(0, y − z). A flat tax has z = 0. With an allowance, people near z bunch at the kink.

Benefits. Revenue R is returned as b = (1−θ)R + θ(R/Y)·y. Workers take the earnings-related part into account when choosing hours, so it offsets the tax at the margin. Budgets balance at a fixed point.

The pivotal voter maximises (1−α)·E[U(x_v)] + α·mean U(x_j), where x is consumption net of effort cost. The expectation is over a log shock to their own earning power, w_v·exp(σζ − σ²/2), integrated by 7-point Gauss–Hermite quadrature.

Benchmark. With a flat tax, flat benefits, no risk and no altruism, the solution matches Meltzer and Richard’s closed form τ* = (1−m)/(1+ε−m), where m is the voter’s income over the mean. Risk aversion is then irrelevant.

Scale. Under these preferences the level of effort cost scales everyone’s output equally and leaves preferred policy unchanged. The effort-cost control therefore moves the curvature, which sets the elasticity ε.

Solver. Grid search over statutory rates 0–95% in 0.5-point steps. In voter-chooses mode it first searches allowance and benefit mix on a coarse grid.
