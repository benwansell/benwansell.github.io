What the numbers mean

Direct share is a university's gross value added (GVA) as a share of its host local authority's GVA. University GVA is income less investment income, donations and bought-in goods and services: in effect, its payroll plus its operating surplus. Local authority GVA is the ONS balanced measure at current prices. Each 2023/24 university figure is compared with 5/12 of calendar 2023 plus 7/12 of 2024 local GVA.

Central share adds the spillovers that stay in the host area: the university's supply chain, spending by staff who live locally, and spending by students who moved there. They come from the ONS 2023 input-output tables, localised for each area with the Flegg location quotient and BRES employment. Upper bound treats every UK-sourced purchase, every staff member and every student as local and additional, using national Type II multipliers. It is a ceiling, not an estimate.

Oxford and Cambridge figures leave out Oxford University Press and Cambridge University Press & Assessment, whose income is mostly earned worldwide. Exeter's Penryn campus counts towards Cornwall. Other multi-campus universities sit at their main campus because no open campus-level data exists.

Comparing across England, Wales and Scotland

Deprivation is the share of an area's small neighbourhoods in its own nation's most deprived 20%, using the English Indices of Deprivation 2025, the Welsh Index of Multiple Deprivation 2019 and the Scottish Index of Multiple Deprivation 2020v2. The three indices use different indicators, years and neighbourhood sizes. A value says how an area sits within its own nation, so compare areas within a nation, not across borders. Ethnicity comes from Census 2021 in England and Wales and Census 2022 in Scotland.

For England only, the English Indices of Deprivation 2025 local authority summaries add the IMD average score, the share of neighbourhoods in England's most deprived 10%, extent (the share of residents living in the most deprived areas), and average scores for each domain: income, employment, education and skills, health and disability, crime, barriers to housing and services, and living environment, plus the child (IDACI) and older-person (IDAOPI) income indices. Income, employment, IDACI and IDAOPI scores are the share of the relevant population that is deprived; the other domain scores are on their own scales, where higher means more deprived.

Local GVA per resident leaves out the City of London, where workplace GVA divided by a population of about 15,000 gives roughly £1m a head.

Politics is the make-up of the district or unitary council in 2026. "Control" means one party holds more than half the seats; otherwise the council is in no overall control. In two-tier areas this is the district council, not the county.

Caveats

  • All figures are estimates. Spillover figures rest on model assumptions: fixed input mixes, no displacement, and staff-residence shares from Census commuting flows taken around lockdown.
  • OUP's GVA is estimated from its turnover. Some press activity is local, so Oxford's and Cambridge's true shares sit between the figures with and without the presses.
  • Survey-based series (APS, ASHE) have wide margins in small authorities and are missing for the Isles of Scilly and the City of London.
