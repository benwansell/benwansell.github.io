Leader maps colour each county by the candidate (or, for the House, the party) ahead there, shaded by their lead over second place. Shift maps show the change in the two-party margin, Democrat minus Republican, in percentage points.
In Senate and Governor races where no Democrat finished in the top two, the leading non-Republican (for example Dan Osborn in Nebraska) is counted on the Democratic side of shift calculations. Counties where either election had a House district without both a Democratic and a Republican candidate are left grey in shift maps and dropped from scatterplots.
House results are every district's vote summed by party within each county. District lines changed in several states between 2024 and 2026, so House shifts mix real movement with new matchups. Chicago is combined with suburban Cook County.
Historical results cover the 48 contiguous states, so Alaska and Hawaii are grey before 2026. Connecticut is mapped by its eight historical counties. The ACS now reports Connecticut by planning region, so its county demographics are built by adding up town estimates, with median income and age interpolated from income and age brackets. Alaska's Chugach and Copper River areas are shown together as Valdez-Cordova.
Tariffs, ICE, gas and grants. ICE arrests run from January 20, 2025 to early August 2026 (Deportation Data Project, from ICE records released under FOIA). "Location known" counts only arrests whose county is named or whose site is specific, about a third of them; the rest are placed by the ICE office's city, which piles arrests onto counties with jails and processing sites. Later deportations count arrested people who later left the country. Tariff increases weight each industry's change in rates by its share of the county's private jobs. "Rates paid" uses duties actually collected (Census trade data), and "announced" uses rates announced through April 9, 2025 (Richmond Fed). Neither counts higher input costs or retaliation, and many small county-industry job counts are estimated. Gas prices are AAA's metro average where AAA reports the county's metro, else its state average. Research grants are NIH, NSF and other federal grants to universities, counted in the county of the university's address. These are county associations, not evidence that any of these changed votes.
